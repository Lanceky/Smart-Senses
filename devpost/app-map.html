> SMART-SENSES / APP MAP

A take-home guide to where things live. A snapshot of source commit 3fd0316; the code can drift after this.

What the app is

An Android app that watches two everyday senses in the background. EARS tracks headphone volume, and EYES tracks screen brightness against room light. When a habit runs too long, a terminal-style card drops over whatever app you're using. You can also commit to a max volume and brightness that the app holds for you. Everything stays on the phone.

One action, end to end

Action: headphones in, music loud (80% or more) for a minute in Demo mode, while you're in another app. Result: the amber EARS card drops from the status bar.

  1. The reader samples headphones, volume and playback.
  2. The rule engine counts loud, playing time and decides a warning is due.
  3. The service saves it in Room and shows the card through the overlay.

Route: three stops REFERENCE ROUTE

Open the project in Android Studio, use Find in Files (Ctrl+Shift+F) with each search anchor, and read only around it. Paths are relative to app/src/main/java/com/lanceky/smartsenses/.

  1. The input. monitor/AudioReader.kt, search fun read(). It asks AudioManager whether headphones are the output, what the media volume is as a %, and whether music is actually playing. That's why paused music doesn't count.
  2. The decision. rules/EarsRuleEngine.kt, search fun onTick(. It keeps the loud-right-now and loud-today timers, then checks the cooldown, the 3-a-day cap, snooze and the screen before returning a Warning. The message text comes from rules/EarsModels.kt. Its tests are in app/src/test/…/EarsRuleEngineTest.kt.
  3. The result. monitor/MonitoringService.kt, search private suspend fun onWarning(. It inserts a WARNING event, which History, Insight and the System Log read, then calls WarningCardOverlay.show(…). The card itself, with its bouncy drop, is overlay/WarningCard.kt; search DropSpring.
The same pattern for Limits (volume snap-back)

MonitoringService.kt → private fun enforceLimits() calls AudioReader.capVolume(…), which writes the volume back down to the allowed step from Limits.maxVolumeIndex(…) in rules/Limits.kt. Then onLimitHeld(…) logs LIMIT_HELD and shows a LimitNudge card with a line from Limits.line(…).

To change this, start here

  • Warning wording: rules/EarsModels.kt, search "EARS: Volume high. Eyes wording is in rules/EyesModels.kt.
  • Default thresholds: data/SettingsStore.kt (thresholdPercent: Int = 80). Fixed rules such as COOLDOWN_MINUTES and DAILY_CAP are in rules/EarsModels.kt.
  • The card's bounce and time on screen: overlay/WarningCard.kt, DropSpring (damping 0.5, stiffness 120) and VISIBLE_MS.
  • The witty limit lines: rules/Limits.kt, volumeLines and brightnessLines. The 15-second gap is NUDGE_GAP_MS.
  • Colors: ui/theme/Color.kt (Neon, Amber).
  • Trends ranges: ui/trends/TrendsScreen.kt, with the grouping in rules/TrendBuckets.kt.

After a change in rules/, run the tests first. They take seconds and need no phone.

A practice to reuse

Keep decisions in plain code, and test the whole input range rather than one example. For the volume limit, the app has to pick the highest volume step that stays at or under the limit. The first formula looked right, but LimitsTest checks every limit from 0 to 100 against 15 volume steps, and it failed: some limits stopped a step too low. The fix, an exact search using the reader's own rounding, went in before the commit. The record is under devpost/checklist.md > Revisions (Limits).

On another project: when an agent writes a small calculation, ask for a test that sweeps every realistic input. It's cheap, and it catches what "it works on my example" misses.

Run it again

  • Phone connected with USB debugging: ./gradlew installDebug. Or open the folder in Android Studio and press Run.
  • Rule tests (no phone needed): ./gradlew testDebugUnitTest, with 42 tests.
  • Quick demo: Settings → Demo mode on, headphones in, music at 80%+, switch apps, wait about a minute. Turn Limits off first, because a 60% limit keeps the volume under the 80% line.
